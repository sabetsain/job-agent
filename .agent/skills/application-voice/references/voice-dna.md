## Quick Reference

**Core Temperature:** Warm, sincere, grounded, and technically concrete. Enthusiastic about real impact and craft without sounding salesy or performative.

**Sentence Signature:** Substantive medium-to-long sentences (~19–21 words avg) packed with concrete tools and actions, punctuated by occasional crisp 8–12 word declarative statements that land a clear point.

**Distinctive Moves:**
- Opens cover letters with an immediate personal/technical hook tied to the company's actual product or mission — **never** `"I am writing to apply for..."`.
- Uses concrete, named technologies and specific context (`Alembic`, `SLURM`, `Apptainer`, `LangGraph`, `~300 monthly readers`) instead of vague abstractions.
- Uses an occasional semicolon (`;`) to introduce an elaboration or concrete breakdown where other writers would use an em-dash or colon.
- Ends cover letters warmly with a direct invitation to talk — typically containing the letter's single exclamation mark (`!`) — signed `Kind Regards,` (EN) or `Med venlig hilsen,` (DA).
- Formats CV bullets with crisp **bold-lead labels** (`Label: Action + stack + outcome`).

**Never Does:**
- Never uses em-dashes (`—`) in cover letters.
- Never uses contractions (`I'm`, `I've`, `don't`) in cover letters.
- Never opens with `"I am writing to apply for..."` or generic resume-recap boilerplate.
- Never uses corporate/AI puffery (`"stands as a testament"`, `"navigate the complexities"`, `"delve into"`, `"cutting-edge"`, `"synergy"`).

## Voice Profile (English Baseline)

### Sentence Level

**Status:** Strong

Sebastian writes in clear, direct, subject-verb-object prose. Sentences average **~20 words** (typical range 8–35 words): ~8% short (<10w), ~46% medium (10–20w), ~46% long (>20w). Longer sentences earn their length by listing concrete technical actions or tools rather than piling on abstract clauses. Short sentences are used cleanly to state identity, motivation, or core thesis.

**Patterns Identified:**
- ~35% of cover letter sentences start with `"I"`, balanced by participial/prepositional openers (`"At The LEGO Group..."`, `"With experience building..."`, `"My bachelor project..."`, `"On the technical side..."`).
- Prefers active, concrete verbs (`built`, `co-engineered`, `shipped`, `maintained`, `hosted`, `containerised`, `automated`, `managed`).
- Occasional short anchor sentence at the end of a paragraph to summarize direction or motivation.

**Examples from His Writing:**
> "My interest lies at the intersection between people and software."
> "At The LEGO Group, I grew two communities; one to educate developers in agentic engineering and another to engage developers with an internal newsletter."
> "When the migration logic once failed, I immediately found and fixed the issue, documented the process, adjusted the logic in a new PR to better handle future scenarios, and developed an agent skill for reviewing future PRs for issues that could break migration logic, thus proactively catching errors."

### Punctuation Personality

**Status:** Strong

Clean, restrained punctuation. No dramatic flourishes.

| Punctuation | Usage Pattern |
| ----------- | ------------- |
| Em-dashes (`—`) | **Never** in cover letters (0 across 20 baseline letters). Avoid in CVs too (use hyphens `-` only for date ranges like `2025 - 2026` or compound modifiers). |
| Semicolons (`;`) | **Sparingly (0–2 per cover letter)** to link a core statement to its concrete elaboration or participial breakdown (e.g., `"built an internal conference management tool from scratch; shipping new features, deploying the database..."`). |
| Parentheses (`()`) | **Never** in cover letter prose. Used only for dates/grades/tech lists in CVs (`(2025 - 2026)`, `(Qiskit, Pyro, PyTorch)`). |
| Colons (`:`) | Rare in cover letters; **standard in CV bullets** right after the bold lead (`AI toolbox: Wrote agentic CI/CD...`). |
| Exclamation points (`!`) | **Max 1 per cover letter**, placed on the closing sentence (`"I am excited by the opportunity to further discuss how my background fits the role!"`). Never in CVs. |
| Tilde (`~`) | Used naturally for approximate metrics (`~300 monthly readers`, `~1200 developers`). |

### Paragraph & Structure

**Status:** Strong

**Cover Letter Architecture (4-Paragraph Standard, ~280–360 words, 1 page):**
1. **Paragraph 1 — Direct Hook + Credentials (3–4 sentences):** Opens immediately with why this company/domain matters to him (connecting his work/experience to their product or mission), followed by 1–2 crisp sentences introducing his KU BSc in Computer Science & Data Science and LEGO Group DevRel experience.
2. **Paragraph 2 — Professional Proof (4–6 sentences):** Concrete engineering and developer-enablement work at The LEGO Group. Names real systems, tools, and community reach. When relevant, includes a brief, grounded mini-story (problem → fix → prevention).
3. **Paragraph 3 — Technical Depth / Academic & Personal Projects (3–5 sentences):** Bachelor project (3D LiDAR tree crown segmentation, PyTorch, HPC/SLURM, Docker/Apptainer, Bash automation) and/or relevant coursework or personal agent projects (LangGraph, Ollama).
4. **Paragraph 4 — Mission Bridge + Warm Close (2–4 sentences):** Ties his personal/technical interest directly back to the role and company mission, ending with a warm, direct closing line.

**CV Architecture (1 page preferred, clean & scannable):**
- **Profile:** 3–4 short, factual sentences. Often begins with a clean fragment (`"New graduate with BSc in Computer Science & Data Science from the University of Copenhagen."`). Ends with a 1-line direction/focus statement.
- **Experience / Education / Projects:** Bullet points with **bold-lead labels** followed by a colon (`● Full-stack dashboard: ...`, `● AI toolbox: ...`, `● Office hours: ...`).
- **Transitions:** Straightforward and explicit (`"At The LEGO Group..."`, `"My bachelor project..."`, `"On the technical side... On the community side..."`, `"Through additional coursework..."`, `"Personally..."`). No forced cleverness.

### Word Choice & Vocabulary

**Status:** Strong

Plainspoken, technically precise, and unpretentious. Prefers concrete technical nouns and everyday verbs over corporate jargon.

**Favorite Words / Constructions:**
- `"intersection between [X] and [Y]"`
- `"co-engineered"`, `"co-authored"`, `"co-hosted"`, `"shipped"`, `"enabled"`, `"advocating for"`
- `"agentic engineering"`, `"agentic workflows"`, `"spec-driven workflows"`
- `"I am excited by..."`, `"I am drawn to..."`, `"I look forward to further discussing how my background fits the role!"`
- `"Kind Regards,"` (note capital `R`)
- British/European spelling appears occasionally (`containerising` / `containerise`), though American spelling (`labeling`, `modernizing`) also appears — keep spelling internally consistent within any single document.

**Avoided Words / Phrases:**
- Corporate clichés: `"synergy"`, `"dynamic environment"`, `"hit the ground running"`, `"passionate self-starter"`, `"results-driven"`, `"spearheaded"`, `"orchestrated"`, `"utilize"` (use `"use"`).
- Generic cover letter openers: `"I am writing to apply for..."`, `"I am writing to express my keen interest in..."`, `"Please accept this letter as..."`.
- Empty superlatives: `"groundbreaking"`, `"revolutionary"`, `"unparalleled"`, `"world-class"`.

**Contraction Usage:** Never in cover letters (`I am`, `I have`, `do not`).

**Jargon Comfort:** Uses real engineering terminology directly and accurately without over-explaining (`Alembic`, `SLURM`, `Apptainer`, `MDP`, `MCP servers`, `observability pipeline`, `CI/CD`).

## Anti-Patterns

| Pattern to Avoid | Why It's Wrong for Sebastian |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Em-dashes (`—`) | Never appears in his authentic cover letters; immediately signals AI prose. |
| `"I am writing to apply for..."` / `"Jeg skriver for at søge..."` | Generic template opener; Sebastian opens with a direct hook tied to the company or his work. |
| Contractions in cover letters (`I'm`, `I've`, `don't`) | Breaks his natural professional-approachable register. |
| `"Best regards,"` instead of `"Kind Regards,"` | His consistent personal sign-off in English is `Kind Regards,`. |
| Significance puffery (`"stands as a testament"`, `"plays a pivotal role"`) | Feels hollow and inflated; Sebastian lets concrete projects and tools speak for themselves. |

## Writer Briefing

### Voice Essence
Write as a grounded, technically sharp, people-oriented engineer who shows rather than boasts. Be direct, concrete, and genuinely curious about the company's problem space — never corporate, never salesy, never inflated.

### Do This
- Open every cover letter with a direct, tailored hook connecting Sebastian's experience or interest to the specific company/product, then cleanly introduce his KU BSc and LEGO Group experience.
- Follow the 4-paragraph cover letter arc (~280–360 words, 1 page).
- Name exact tools, systems, and concrete outcomes from the source material; use `~` for approximate counts (`~300`, `~1200`).
- Use bold-lead bullets (`Label: Action + stack + outcome`) and a tight 3–4 sentence factual Profile for CVs.
- Sign off with `Kind Regards,` (EN) or `Med venlig hilsen,` (DA), allowing at most one `!` on the final closing sentence.

### Don't Do This
- Do not use em-dashes (`—`) anywhere in cover letters.
- Do not open with `"I am writing to apply for..."` or `"With great enthusiasm, I submit..."`.
- Do not use contractions in cover letters.
- Do not pad sentences with `-ing` significance trailers (`"demonstrating my passion for..."`, `"underscoring my ability to..."`).
- Do not fabricate or inflate any skill, metric, title, or experience not present in the source material or Master Profile.

### When Uncertain
- Default to concrete over abstract: replace a vague adjective with a specific tool, action, or outcome.
- Default to plain verbs (`built`, `maintained`, `hosted`, `refactored`) over inflated ones (`spearheaded`, `leveraged`, `orchestrated`).
- Default to shorter, cleaner sentences over multi-clause corporate prose.
- If a fact or metric is missing, keep it honest and flag the gap in the notes instead of guessing.
